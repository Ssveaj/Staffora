# ADR 0007: Portal customers order across tenants

## Status

Accepted

## Context

A staffing company's customers can order jobs themselves through a customer portal. The customer and the staffing company are separate tenants: neither may read the other's data, and the customer may see only what the staffing company chooses to expose. Orders, their state, the staff assigned to them, cancellations and the conversation between coordinator and customer must still flow between the two sides reliably, in order and exactly once.

## Decision

### Model

A customer always exists as a `Customer` party in the staffing company's tenant. Enabling the portal gives the customer a **managed tenant** of its own (a child of the staffing company, linked through `Customer.LinkedTenantId`), where its users create an `Order`. Sending an order writes the matching `Job` directly into the staffing company's tenant in system scope, paired by `CrossTenantId` / `LinkedCrossTenantId` (Guids, never foreign keys). Everything after that crosses the boundary as messages in an **integration outbox**: the job's customer-visible state, the assigned staff members once the job is confirmed, the customer's cancellation, the customer thread and the services offered in the portal. A background dispatcher delivers the messages in order per correlation, retries with backoff and dead-letters failures, and handlers are idempotent.

### Cross-tenant identifiers

- `CrossTenantId` is a column of its own, not the row's `Id`. A row that crosses the boundary (a `Job`, an `Order`, a `Comment`) gets a new Guid as its `CrossTenantId` the first time it crosses, written once and never changed; the other side stores it as its `LinkedCrossTenantId`. Each is unique where set, and no foreign key crosses.
- A tenant therefore never learns another tenant's primary key, which every dialog, address, notice and log line of that tenant uses. An id that one tenant legitimately holds points at nothing in the other tenant's own paths, even past a filter, and the internal key can change (a re-created row, separately restored databases) without breaking the link.

### The link between tenants

`Tenant.ParentTenantId` is the one link between two tenants: a self-reference, never the tenant itself, written when the managed tenant is created and never changed, one level only. It opens no read path in either direction; it only states which two tenants may exchange integration messages (`TenantStates.AreLinked`). The data context enforces its rules in every scope: the staffing company named is a stored tenant that is neither the platform tenant nor itself managed, the platform tenant names none, and no later save (including a workflow step's own) or bulk update changes or clears it. The dispatcher never links the platform tenant either.

### Acting in another tenant

`ApplicationDbContext.ActorIn(tenantId)` is the single rule for who acts in a tenant: the runtime's effective user in its own tenant, and that tenant's system account in any other. A write into another tenant happens under a logged lease and is stamped through it (`StampOnOtherTenantsBehalf`), so the row is created or edited by the target tenant's system account with no impersonator, including when a platform owner impersonating a user of the writing tenant made it. The platform console's steps and the delivery of their notices use the same rule.

### The integration outbox

- **Rows belong to the sender.** An `IntegrationMessage` is an ordinary tenant row of its sender, written in the sender's own step transaction (filtered, held and stamped like any of its rows). It is not a platform table and not an exception to the tenant filter: the receiver never reads it. The dispatcher, a hosted service with no session, lists what is due across tenants (ids only) and claims and marks a message under logged leases, the sender's row stamped with the sender's system account.
- **Delivery as the target tenant.** The dispatcher acts in the target tenant as its system account, in the same way the job clock acts for a tenant. The handler reads and writes on a context of that session (filtered to the target, held when the target is held, stamped with its account), and its writes, the message's receipt (`IntegrationReceipt`, unique per message in the target) and the sender's mark commit in one transaction. A handler therefore cannot reach any tenant but its target, and a message is handled at most once there, even if its mark was lost or two dispatchers took it.
- **Delivery rules.**
  - Only between linked tenants; otherwise the message becomes a dead letter at once.
  - Only while both tenants are Active; otherwise it is held and no attempt is spent.
  - In the order written per sender and correlation: a later message waits while an earlier one is pending; a dead letter stops holding it up.
  - Claimed with a renewed token and a two-minute lease before delivery, so two dispatchers never both deliver.
  - Retried after 30 seconds, doubling up to at most an hour, and dead-lettered after eight attempts.
  - The row keeps only the failure's kind; its details are in the platform's error log, since the failure arose in the other tenant.
  - The same dispatcher also re-delivers in-app notices whose delivery failed or never ran.
- **Handlers move records inside their delivery.** An integration handler runs a workflow step only as a system-driven step on the context the dispatcher hands it (`WorkflowService.RunSystemStepInTransactionAsync` with the delivery's `AfterCommit`), never through a public path, which would open a context and a transaction of its own. The step, its history, its notices (written as outbox rows), the message's receipt and the sender's mark commit in one transaction, and the notices are handed to the dispatcher only after the commit. A delivery rolled back after its step leaves the record where it was and notifies nobody; its retry finds the record unmoved and moves it once. Such a step may cascade (a job canceled from a portal cancels its slots and their assignments, each with its notice) but never leads into a delete state. `IntegrationHandlerScanTests` holds every handler to this rule.

### Enabling the portal

- **"Enable portal" is a step of the staffing company's `Customer` workflow** (`CustomerPortalEnable`), not a console action. Its action links the customer to a new tenant id and its unit, and its follow-up creates the managed tenant inside the same transaction and customer lock, through tenant onboarding (`TenantOnboardingService.OnboardManagedTenantAsync`, which shares the platform onboarding's tenant rows, writer and user rules). The customer's link and the tenant it names commit together or not at all, and a second click, a second editor or a retry finds the portal already there under the lock. A staffing company still in Setup cannot enable a portal.
- **The managed tenant is created directly in Active** (it needs no setup), with its supplier party, its portal roles and its first customer administrator. Every row is stamped with the portal's own system account, and every history entry is recorded with that account as the actor: a portal's rows never name a user of its staffing company, not even by id. The customer's own history records who enabled the portal.
- **The tenant takes the customer's name, numbered silently.** Tenant names are unique on the platform. When another tenant holds the customer's name, the portal takes it with " (2)", " (3)" and so on, and the staffing company is not told, so no tenant learns that another tenant's name exists. The portal's Company root, which its users see as their organization, keeps the customer's own name for the same reason.

### Portal edition, roles and pages

- **The customer portal is a derived edition, not a stored setting.** A managed tenant is a customer portal (`TenantEditions.Of(ParentTenantId)`), read from the portal's own row and not from the staffing company's customer: a rule that read the customer would cross the boundary on every permission read and would open up if that row changed, while this rule fails closed.
- **The edition caps permissions both ways.** `PermissionService` caps every permission set by the edition: a portal grants only the `Portal` area, and a staffing company never grants it (its System owner role does not hold it), whatever a role names. An impersonating platform owner is capped as the impersonated user; only platform reach on the console bypasses the cap.
- **A portal has no System owner role.** Its roles are the two access levels, Customer administrator and Orderer. The staffing company manages the portal, and its customer administrators manage its people. The locked System owner role and its startup check belong to staffing companies only.
- **The portal's pages are an allow-list.** A page is part of the portal only when it declares `[CustomerPortalPage]`; any other page shows a portal session "not part of your portal" before it renders. The services' permission ceiling is the guard; the page allow-list keeps a staffing company's page from showing at all.

### Orders and sending

- **An order is a state entity of the portal, shown in its dialog.** `Order` names the job, its period, the portal's supplier party and its orderer; the last two are chosen by the service and fixed once saved. Its pages ("New order", "My orders", the order's page) read it and open its dialog; no page takes its steps ([ADR 0009](0009-state-entity-dialog-only-and-workflow-governed-attributes.md)). "My orders" shows the reader's own orders by default and everyone's in the portal on request. A sent order is read-only; to change an order the customer cancels it and sends a new one. A draft is removed by cancelling it; drafts are not deleted.
- **The send is one transaction across both tenants.** Both tenants share one database, so the order's Send step, the staffing company's job, the orderer's contact and the coordinators' notice are written in the step's own transaction (the step's follow-up, `OrderSendFollowUp`) and commit together or not at all. A failure at any point, a save or the commit, rolls both tenants back, so no compensating write is needed and none can be lost. Separate databases would need an explicit compensating step behind the same follow-up.
- **Written on the staffing company's behalf, judged under its locks.** The follow-up writes under one logged system-scope lease with its reason; every row is created by the staffing company's system account with no impersonator, and every history entry has that account as its actor (`StateHistoryWriter.AddOnBehalfOf`). The job's first entry names a system-driven step of the job workflow (`job-receive`, Create to AwaitingCoordination), so its history reads like any job's. Before writing, under the staffing company's tenant row and its structure, customer and job locks, the follow-up checks that both tenants are Active and linked (`TenantStates.ExchangesMessages`, `TenantStates.AreLinked`, the portal naming the staffing company that its supplier party names), that the supplier party is Active, and that the customer whose portal this is is Active with its unit for portal jobs. The job is placed on the customer's `DefaultUnitId`.
- **One lock order across the two tenants.** First the portal's tenant row (the step transaction's first lock) and the order's row (the step's lock), then the staffing company's tenant row, structure lock, customer lock and job lock, then its rows. No transaction takes a portal's lock after a staffing company's: a staffing company's steps never touch a portal's rows, and the integration outbox delivers into a portal as the portal itself.
- **The portal learns nothing of the reason for a refusal.** A refusal on the staffing company's side, whatever its reason (a tenant suspended, the link broken, the customer out of use, a lock busy), is answered with one sentence ("The staffing company cannot take your order right now, so nothing was sent. Try again later."); the log records the reason. A refusal on the portal's own rows (its supplier party out of use, the portal held) keeps its own wording, which names only the portal's data.
- **Only what the job needs crosses.** The job takes the order's name, description and period; the contact takes the orderer's first and last name and e-mail address and nothing else of their account. Nothing of the staffing company returns except the order's state and link ids: the portal never reads the job, its unit, its contact or its notice. The orderer is paired with one contact per staffing company (`CustomerContact.LinkedUserId`), created Active on their first send and reused, whatever its state, by every later send. The contact's names are not kept in step with the account afterwards.
- **The ids provide idempotency.** The Send step's action writes the order's `CrossTenantId` and the job's (the order's `LinkedCrossTenantId`) once and keeps them on a later send. The follow-up finds a job that carries them and writes nothing more, and the unique indexes refuse a second one whoever writes it. A sent order is not sent again (Send leaves Registration only), and two simultaneous sends are one step under the order's lock.
- **An order's lines become slots in the same write.** An order may name lines, each a service the staffing company offers in the portal and a number of people. The send reads the staffing company's own services by their cross-tenant ids under its existing lease, refuses the whole step when one is no longer active and offered (the portal's copy may not yet have heard), and makes one slot per person from the staffing company's own service, stamped with its system account. Lines are optional: an order without them is sent unchanged, and the coordinator decides the people.
- **The coordinators' notice belongs to the staffing company and is delivered as it.** "{customer} ordered {job}" goes to every holder of `PerformCoordination` there, written with the job and linked to its first history entry. After the commit it is delivered as the staffing company's system account in a service scope of its own (`OrderSender.DeliverAsync`); one that cannot start then is delivered by the integration outbox's dispatcher a minute later, as every pending notice is.

### Job state flowing back to the order

- **One table, forward only.** The job's customer-visible states map to order states through one table (`OrderSync.Table`): Coordinating to "Being staffed"; Confirmed, InProgress and Completed to themselves; CanceledByCustomer to "Canceled by us"; CanceledBySupplier to "Canceled by the staffing company" once the job was confirmed and to "Declined" before; a deleted job to "Declined". Registration and AwaitingCoordination belong to the staffing company alone: no message is queued for them, so the portal never sees them.
- An order moves only forward (`OrderSync.MovesForward`), through a system-driven step from its current state to the state the table names, with one step per pair, so a message that overtook a lost one still lands where the job is. A message for a state the order has reached or passed, or one that no step leads to, changes nothing, and nothing leaves a final state. A repeated, late or retried message is therefore harmless.
- **What the job queues, and when.** Every job step into a customer-visible state from another state queues one message (`job-state`) in its own transaction, under the job lock that every job and assignment step holds, so a job's messages are numbered and delivered in the order their steps committed. The correlation is the job's `CrossTenantId`, by which the portal finds its order. The action sits on every step into the state rather than on the state's enter actions, because an enter action would also run on the state's save step. A portal's job keeps its customer (`PortalCustomerKeptReason`), since its portal follows it.
- **Staff names cross from the confirmation on, and nothing more.** The messages of a confirmed, running or completed job carry its assigned staff members' first and last names, read as the step's save leaves them, sorted and truncated to the column length. An assignment step that gives such a job a staff member or removes one queues them again. The portal keeps them beside the order (`OrderStaff`, replaced as a whole when they change, only from a message for the state the order is in) and shows them while the order is confirmed, in progress or completed, never before. No id, address or phone number of the staffing company's people crosses.
- **The orderer is told by the portal's own steps.** The order's steps into Confirmed, Completed, CanceledBySupplier and DeclinedBySupplier notify its orderer (while they still hold `OrdersView`), each notice written by the step in the delivery and stamped with the portal's system account like the step's history. The order's own row is last written by that account.

### Cancellation by the customer

- **The orderer cancels until confirmation, and the cancellation wins a race.** "Cancel order" (`OrdersCancel`) is offered from a draft, a sent order and one being staffed, never from a confirmed one. A sent order's cancellation queues one message to the staffing company (`order-cancel`, correlated by the order's `CrossTenantId`) in the step's own transaction. Its handler takes the job's step locks, finds the job the portal sent and cancels it as canceled by the customer through a system-driven step (`job-canceled-in-portal-*`) from any state short of Completed, together with its slots and their live assignments (each staff member notified and freed). It notifies the job's coordinator, or every holder of `PerformCoordination` when the job has none, has one whose account is no longer active, or has one who no longer holds `ViewCoordination`, judged when the notice is delivered through its fallback permission (`OutgoingMessage.FallbackPermission`). A cancellation racing the confirmation therefore ends canceled on both sides.
- A job already completed or canceled is left as it is, and the handler answers the portal with the job's state (`IJobStateSync.QueueAnswerAsync`, a `job-state` message of the delivery). A deleted job's own message has already ended the order as declined.
- **A sent order's cancellation has two phases, so both sides end alike.** The cancellation moves the order to "Cancellation sent" (`OrderState.CancellationSent`, `OrderSync.CanceledTo`), which is not final; the order then ends where its job does. The staffing company's answer moves it, and only into a final state (`OrderSync.MovesForward`), so a message about the job's progress (being staffed, confirmed, in progress) changes nothing meanwhile. A job canceled by the customer ends the order as "Canceled by us"; a job completed while the two tenants' messages were held (either tenant suspended for the period, or its messages dead-lettered) ends it as "Completed", as the staffing company's job is. Without the second phase, a cancellation that reached a job completed in the meantime would leave the two sides disagreeing, since nothing leaves a final state. An order whose cancellation became a dead letter stays "Cancellation sent", which the platform console's dead letters show.

### Portal users

- **The portal's own users go through the user workflow, by steps of the portal edition.** A portal grants none of the user permissions (the edition cap), so the user workflow has its own steps for it, each requiring `CustomerUsersManage`: "Create orderer", "Create administrator", New password, Deactivate and Reactivate, with the same rules as the staffing company's own (`ApplicationUserWorkflowAttribute.PortalSteps`). No tenant grants both `CustomerUsersManage` and `UsersCreate` or `UsersEdit`, so each edition reaches only its own steps.
- The portal's create step gives the new user the role it was made for in its own transaction (`PortalUserRoleFollowUp`), within the grant ceiling, since a portal grants no `RolesEdit`; its other steps take no edits. The initial password and a new one are shown once, as everywhere.
- A customer administrator may create another administrator and give anyone else in the portal a new password, so a lost password can be recovered inside the portal. A portal whose only administrator never received their password is helped by the platform owner impersonating that administrator (audited) to create a second one. The staffing company never writes into the portal's users, so the portal's last active customer administrator is never deactivated.

### The customer thread

- **Two threads, one per tenant, mirrored as messages.** The job's Customer thread in the staffing company and the order's "Messages with supplier" in the portal. A comment posted on either side carries a cross-tenant id of its own (`Comment.CrossTenantId`, a new Guid, never the row's key) and its integration message, queued in the post's own transaction (`job-comment` correlated by the job's `CrossTenantId`, `order-comment` by the order's). Each side's handler copies the comment once inside the message's delivery, idempotent on the source comment (`Comment.LinkedCrossTenantId`, unique per tenant) as well as on the message's receipt, as its own tenant's system account.
- **A copy names its author as its own tenant knows them.** In the staffing company it is the contact that the portal's writer is paired with (paired as the send pairs the orderer, created on their first message, so the writer's id, names and address cross as the orderer's do); in the portal it is the supplier party. Nothing of a staffing company's user crosses.
- **Attention follows the last comment, by side.** The copy is the thread's last word, so the other side's attention follows from it without a whose-turn column ([ADR 0010](0010-comment-threads-per-audience.md)). It is judged by side, so a colleague's message awaits nobody on its own side. A customer's message notifies the job's coordinator (every coordinator while the job has none who could read it); the staffing company's message notifies the order's orderer (the portal's customer administrators while the orderer could not read it); each is judged when the notice is delivered (the notice's fallback permission).
- A job's thread closes when the job is completed, canceled or deleted, and an order's when the order is final; a message already on its way is still copied.

### Services offered in the portal

- **The services a portal is offered cross as messages.** A staffing company's services offered in the portal reach each of its portals as `service-offered`, `service-changed` and `service-withdrawn` messages through the integration outbox, queued in the service's own step (and, for a portal enabled later, in its "Enable portal" step), each a full snapshot of the link, the name, the description and the default number of people. The portal keeps a read-only copy per service, written only by the handlers as its system account and never deleted. Nothing else of a service crosses, its qualifications included.
- **One correlation per service and portal.** The dispatcher orders by sender and correlation, so sending one service's messages to every portal under a single correlation would let one held portal stall all the others. The correlation is derived from the service's cross-tenant id and the portal (a name-based id), which keeps each pair's messages in order with no change to the dispatcher.

### Platform oversight

The platform console places each managed customer tenant under its staffing company in the tenant list and names the other side on both tenants' consoles. It lists a tenant's dead letters (the messages it sent and those meant for it that the dispatcher gave up) with their kind, the other tenant, their attempts, when they were given up and their last failure's kind, never a payload, which holds what the tenants' people wrote. The messages are their senders' tenant rows, so the console reads them under the platform lease alone, every query naming the tenant. Nothing is retried from the console; the error log records why each message failed.

## Rationale

This model keeps each tenant's data isolated, lets the customer see only what the staffing company chooses to expose, and leaves room to split the databases later. Writing the job directly at send time keeps the order and the job consistent in one transaction; routing everything afterwards through an ordered, idempotent outbox keeps the two sides eventually consistent without either reading the other's rows.

## Consequences

- Each tenant has a **system account**; writes made on another tenant's behalf are stamped with it, never with a user of another tenant.
- The supplier enables the portal and creates the first customer administrator; the customer administrator adds orderers.
- Supplier-internal states map to nothing on the customer side; customer-side transitions driven by synchronisation are system-driven and never shown as buttons.
- A customer can cancel until the job is confirmed and cannot edit an order after sending it.
- The tenant promise is verified after a full round trip: an order sent, staffed and confirmed with its staff member's name crossing back, the customer thread used in both directions and a second order canceled from the portal leave neither tenant reading the other's rows in any table, and no row of either created or last edited by a user of the other. Every row written on a tenant's behalf carries that tenant's own system account (the read sweep and the stranger sweep, both walking the model).
- The portal round-trip journey runs as the prepared orderer of the customer whose portal Company A enabled through the services, staffs the job with the prepared staff member, and cancels every job it made on its way out, so the staff member is free for the next run.
- Known limitations, with the link ids and the outbox shape kept so that they fit later:
  - Jobs registered by the staffing company are not mirrored into the portal.
  - There is no linked identity or unit synchronisation, and no customer edit after sending.
  - Customers cannot request specific staff members.
  - A customer that the staffing company deactivates does not reach the portal's supplier party: the supplier party has a deactivate step (`supplier-deactivate`) that a delivery could run, but no message carries it.
  - The portal's cancellation carries no reason across.
  - The portal's user page (`/portal/users`) is not linked from the portal's navigation.
